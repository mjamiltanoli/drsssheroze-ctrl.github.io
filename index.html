Box 1: International Law & Artificial Intelligence - Legal Basis

This proposal is presented under De Lege Ferenda (law as it should be).

It does not assert that AI governance has already become a separate rule of Jus Cogens under Article 53 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties.

It invites examination whether existing peremptory norms require further development to address existential AI risks.

Reference: VCLT Article 53 - Jus Cogens

Box 2: Five Evidence-Supported AI Risk Scenarios
  • Loss of meaningful human control
  • Threat to State sovereignty and critical infrastructure
  • Systematic human rights impact
  • Concentration of unaccountable power
  • International peace and security implications

These are risk scenarios for assessment, not claims of proven incidents.

Presented for consideration under UN Charter Article 39 framework without suggesting a determination has been made.

Box 3: Two Internets + Neutral Satellite - Technical Concept

Proposed architecture: Separation of critical sovereign infrastructure network from public internet, with a neutral satellite under UN coordination concept.

This is a proposal for independent technical feasibility examination, not a claim of existing implementation.

Competent bodies:

  • ITU Constitution Article 1 - Telecom regulation
  • Outer Space Treaty Articles 1-2 - Space province of all mankind
  • UNESCO AI Ethics Recommendation 2021
Box 4: Protection of Human Life

Proposed alignment with Article 6 ICCPR (Right to Life) and UDHR.

No new human rights claim, request for interpretive guidance on AI-related threats to life.

Reference: ICCPR Article 6

Box 5: State Sovereignty

Based on UN Charter Article 2(1) sovereign equality, Article 2(4) prohibition of force, and Montevideo Convention 1933 criteria.

AI systems that undermine sovereign control require international assessment.

Reference: UN Charter Article 2

Box 6: Critical Infrastructure Protection

Proposal for international cooperation to protect power, health, financial, and communication infrastructure from uncontrolled AI interference.

Reference: UN GGE norms on responsible State behaviour in cyberspace.

Box 7: Head of State - International Representation

This does not claim Head of State immunity or status creates new law.

It proposes that Heads of State, as highest representatives under international law, should receive assessment of AI existential risks for national security consideration.

Reference: Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, customary international law on representation.

Box 8: UN Charter Article 39 - International Peace and Security
UIC Correction: This proposal does NOT activate Article 39.
Only the Security Council may determine threat to peace, breach of peace, or act of aggression.
This is a request for assessment whether evidence-supported AI risks may warrant consideration within Article 39 framework.
Box 9: UN Charter Article 99 & 71 - Institutional Route
UIC Correction Article 99: Secretary-General may bring any matter threatening international peace to Security Council if threshold independently satisfied.
We request consideration, not action.
UIC Correction Article 71: ECOSOC may make arrangements for consultation with NGOs per Resolution 1996/31.
We recognise Article 71 does not confer consultative status on individual author.
We request guidance on appropriate route.

References:

  • UN Charter Articles 71, 99
  • ECOSOC Resolution 1996/31

Box 11: Final Legal Assessment - Response to 6 UIC Questions

Q1: Does VCLT Art53 create new Jus Cogens right for AI?
Answer: No. Proposal under De Lege Ferenda, invites examination, does not assert new rule established.

Q2: Does this activate UN Charter Art39?
Answer: No. Only Security Council may determine. Request for assessment only.

Q3: Does Head of State establish new rule?
Answer: No. Proposes consideration by highest representatives, no new immunity or status claim.

Q4: Two Internets - supporting text, technical feasibility, existing agreements?
Answer: Provided - ITU Constitution Art1, Outer Space Treaty Art1-2, UNESCO AI Ethics. Feasibility to be examined by competent bodies, not claimed as feasible unilaterally.
